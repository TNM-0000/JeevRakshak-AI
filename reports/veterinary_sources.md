## 2. Priority Disease Profiles

### 2.1. Lumpy Skin Disease (LSD)
- **Etiological Agent**: Lumpy Skin Disease Virus (LSDV), genus *Capripoxvirus*, family *Poxviridae* (double-stranded DNA virus). Closely related to Sheeppox and Goatpox viruses.
- **Susceptible Host Species**: Cattle (*Bos taurus* and *Bos indicus*) and Asian water buffalo (*Bubalus bubalis*). Morbidity is substantially higher in European crossbred cattle than indigenous zebu breeds.
- **Incubation Period**: 4 to 14 days under field conditions (up to 28 days experimentally).
- **Morbidity & Mortality**: Morbidity ranges from 10% to 45% (reaching >70% in naive crossbred herds); mortality is generally low (1% to 5%) but can spike due to secondary bacterial pneumonia and severe necrotic sloughing.
- **Pathognomonic & Clinical Signs**:
  - Initial phase: High biphasic pyrexia (40.0°C – 41.5°C / 104°F – 107°F), sharp drop in milk yield, marked depression, anorexia.
  - Cutaneous phase: Rapid eruption of firm, well-demarcated, circular nodules (0.5 cm to 5.0 cm diameter) involving the full thickness of the skin and subcutis across the neck, brisket, limbs, udder, perineum, and head.
  - Progressive phase: Nodules may develop central depression, necrotic plugs ("sit-fasts"), ulcerate, or slough leaving deep purulent craters prone to myiasis (maggot infestation).
  - Systemic signs: Marked enlargement of superficial lymph nodes (prescapular and precrural), unilateral or bilateral limb edema, dewlap edema, mucoid to mucopurulent nasal and ocular discharge, keratitis.
- **Transmission Mechanics**:
  - Primarily mechanical vector-borne transmission via blood-feeding arthropods: biting flies (*Stomoxys calcitrans*), mosquitoes (*Aedes aegypti*, *Culex quinquefasciatus*), and hard ticks (*Rhipicephalus appendiculatus*, *Amblyomma hebraeum*).
  - Minor direct transmission via shared troughs, saliva, milk, or infected semen.
- **Indian Prevention & Control Standards (DAHD/ICAR Guidelines)**:
  - Vaccination: Heterologous Goat Pox Vaccine (Uttarkashi strain at 10^3 TCID50 for cattle >4 months) or indigenous homologous *Lumpi-ProVacInd* (developed jointly by ICAR-NRCE Hisar and ICAR-IVRI).
  - Biosecurity: Strict movement restriction of cattle within a 10 km radius of epicenters, application of ectoparasiticides (deltamethrin/cypermethrin), separation of affected animals in vector-proof sheds.
  - Notification: Notifiable disease in India; mandatory reporting to the District Veterinary Officer (DVO).

### 2.2. Foot-and-Mouth Disease (FMD)
- **Etiological Agent**: Foot-and-Mouth Disease Virus (FMDV), genus *Aphthovirus*, family *Picornaviridae* (positive-sense single-stranded RNA virus). Endemic serotypes circulating in India are **O, A, and Asia-1** (Serotype C has not been detected in India since 1995).
- **Susceptible Host Species**: All cloven-hoofed domestic and wild animals: cattle, buffalo, sheep, goats, pigs, and wild ungulates.
- **Incubation Period**: 2 to 14 days (typically 3 to 5 days).
- **Morbidity & Mortality**: Morbidity approaches 100% in naive herds; adult mortality is typically low (<2% to 5%), but calf mortality can exceed 20% to 50% due to acute non-suppurative viral myocarditis ("tiger heart").
- **Pathognomonic & Clinical Signs**:
  - Hyperthermia (40.0°C – 41.0°C / 104°F – 106°F), severe depression, complete loss of appetite.
  - Oral lesions: Fluid-filled vesicles (blisters) on the dorsum of the tongue, dental pad, gums, inner cheeks, and muzzle that quickly rupture, leaving painful erosions and raw red ulcers.
  - Characteristic behavior: Profuse, stringy, ropy salivation (drooling), smacking of lips, tongue chewing.
  - Podal (Foot) lesions: Vesicles on the coronary band, heel bulbs, and interdigital cleft, leading to acute lameness, shaking of feet, recumbency, and in severe cases, claw exungulation (sloughing of hoof horn).
  - Teat lesions: Vesicles on teats causing painful suckling, mastitis, and severe irreversible reduction in lactation.
- **Transmission Mechanics**:
  - Highly contagious via direct aerosol transmission (inhalation of respiratory droplets over long distances), direct contact with vesicle fluid/saliva/milk/dung, and indirect mechanical fomites (farm vehicles, footwear, equipment, feed).
- **Indian Prevention & Control Standards (NADCP / DAHD)**:
  - National Programme: National Animal Disease Control Programme (NADCP) for FMD and Brucellosis (100% centrally funded).
  - Vaccination Protocol: Biannual compulsory mass vaccination of all cattle and buffaloes with high-potency oil-adjuvanted trivalent (O, A, Asia-1) inactivated vaccine. Ear tagging with 12-digit INAPH barcode.
  - Biosecurity & Disinfection: Foot baths using 4% sodium carbonate (washing soda) or 0.2% citric acid or 1:100 potassium permanganate solution.

### 2.3. Anthrax
- **Etiological Agent**: *Bacillus anthracis* (Gram-positive, spore-forming, rod-shaped bacterium). Produces lethal toxin and edema toxin.
- **Zoonotic Status**: High-consequence **zoonosis**; causes cutaneous, gastrointestinal, or pulmonary anthrax in humans.
- **Susceptible Host Species**: All mammals; ruminants (cattle, sheep, goats, buffalo) are extremely susceptible.
- **Clinical Presentation**:
  - Peracute Form: Sudden collapse and death within 1 to 2 hours of apparent health, often discovered dead in the morning without prior warning signs.
  - Acute Form: High fever (41.5°C), tremors, dyspnea, colic, mucosal congestion, bloody diarrhea, dark uncoagulated blood exuding from natural orifices (nostrils, mouth, rectum, vulva).
  - Pathognomonic post-mortem signs: **Complete absence of rigor mortis**, rapid bloating/putrefaction, dark pitch-black non-clotting blood oozing from body openings.
- **Critical Safety Instruction**:
  - **NEVER CONDUCT A POST-MORTEM EXAMINATION** if anthrax is suspected. Exposure to atmospheric oxygen induces bacterial sporulation, contaminating the soil for 40–50+ years.
  - Immediate notification to veterinary authorities; carcass disposal by deep burial (>6 feet deep) enclosed in quicklime (calcium oxide) or complete incineration on site.

### 2.4. Haemorrhagic Septicaemia (HS)
- **Etiological Agent**: *Pasteurella multocida* subsp. *multocida* (predominantly serotype B:2 in India and Asia; Heddleston Type 2).
- **Susceptible Host Species**: Buffaloes (most vulnerable with highest fatality) and cattle.
- **Incubation Period**: 1 to 3 days.
- **Epidemiological Risk Context**: Strongly correlated with the onset of monsoon/rainy season, high humidity, waterlogging, transport stress, and nutritional exhaustion.
- **Clinical Signs**:
  - Sudden onset of high fever (40.5°C – 41.5°C), lethargy, anorexia.
  - Distinctive hot, painful, subcutaneous inflammatory edema extending from the throat, submandibular space, down the brisket and neck.
  - Severe respiratory distress: dyspnea, extended neck, open-mouth breathing, tongue protrusion, loud inspiratory stridor, profuse salivation.
  - Rapid septicemia leading to recumbency and death within 12 to 36 hours.
- **Control**: Pre-monsoon prophylactic vaccination with alum-precipitated or oil-adjuvant HS vaccine; prompt early parenteral antimicrobial therapy (oxytetracycline, trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, or third-generation cephalosporins) before extensive edema sets in.

### 2.5. Black Quarter (BQ) / Blackleg
- **Etiological Agent**: *Clostridium chauvoei* (Gram-positive, anaerobic spore-forming bacterium).
- **Susceptible Host Species**: Young cattle between 6 months and 2 years of age in thriving, good nutritional condition.
- **Clinical Signs**:
  - Sudden severe lameness in one hind or forelimb, profound toxemia, high fever (41°C).
  - Development of hot, painful subcutaneous swellings over heavily muscled areas (rump, thigh, shoulder, neck).
  - Pathognomonic palpation sign: **Subcutaneous crepitation** (distinct crackling / bubbling sound and sensation under hand pressure caused by gas production in muscular necrosis).
  - Affected skin turns dry, cold, leathery, and dark purple/black. Muscular incision reveals black, porous muscle tissue smelling of rancid butter (butyric acid).
  - Fatality rate approaches 95–100% without immediate high-dose antibiotic therapy in early stages.
- **Control**: Annual pre-monsoon vaccination with polyvalent or monovalent *C. chauvoei* bacterin; burning/deep burial of carcasses.

### 2.6. Brucellosis
- **Etiological Agent**: *Brucella abortus* (Gram-negative, facultative intracellular coccobacillus).
- **Zoonotic Status**: High-consequence **zoonosis**; causes undulant fever (Malta fever), arthritis, and chronic fatigue in humans.
- **Susceptible Host Species**: Cattle and buffalo; transmits to humans through unpasteurized milk or assisting infected calvings/placental handling.
- **Clinical Signs**:
  - Third-trimester abortion storms (characteristically between month 6 and month 8 of gestation).
  - Retained placenta (retentio secundinarum), persistent purulent metritis, subsequent permanent or temporary infertility.
  - In breeding bulls: Orchitis, epididymitis, testicular swelling, and viable shedding of bacteria in semen.
  - Hygromas (fluid swellings) in carpal joints in chronic herd infection.
- **Control (NADCP Guidelines)**:
  - Compulsory one-time lifetime vaccination of all female bovine calves aged 4 to 8 months with *Brucella abortus* S19 viable attenuated vaccine.
  - Serological screening (Rose Bengal Plate Test - RBPT, ELISA) and milk ring testing (MRT).

## 3. Mandatory Protocol for AI System Outputs

1. **No Synthetic Diagnosis**: The AI is prohibited from outputting "You animal has X disease." It must output "Observations are consistent with clinical signs of X disease. High risk indicated by visual lesions."
2. **Mandatory Veterinary Referral**: Every high-confidence or high-severity assessment must immediately output the emergency contact action: "Contact your nearest Gram Panchayat Veterinary Assistant Surgeon (VAS) or Pashu Chikitsalaya immediately."
3. **Biosecurity First**: In suspected cases of FMD, LSD, or Anthrax, the system must prioritize immediate containment guidance (isolation of sick animals, disinfection, no carcass opening) before any secondary advice.
